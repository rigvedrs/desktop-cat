YOUR DESKTOP CAT
================

Double-click DesktopCat.exe. A cat appears near the bottom-right of your
screen and stays on top of whatever you're doing.

There is nothing to install.


THE FIRST TIME YOU OPEN IT
--------------------------

Two things may happen. Both are normal.

  * A blue screen saying "Windows protected your PC".
    Click "More info", then "Run anyway".
    Windows says this about any program that isn't from a big company.

  * A pause of five or ten seconds before anything appears.
    A small "Desktop Cat" box shows up while it gets ready.
    Please don't double-click again while you wait.


PLAYING WITH IT
---------------

  Type anything            the cat taps its paws along with you
  Move your mouse around   its eyes follow the cursor
  Move your mouse over it  it closes its eyes and hearts float up
  Click it                 a quick pet
  Drag it                  move it anywhere you like
  Leave it alone a while   it falls asleep

Right-click the cat for the menu. You can pick a different cat (there are
four), switch the eyes between round and slim, change the size, turn the
cursor-following off if it's staring at you, and turn on "Start with
Windows" so it's there every morning.

If the cat is ever in your way, turn on "Click through" in that menu. It
stays visible but your mouse goes straight past it.

To close it: right-click the cat, then Quit.
If you can't find the cat, click the small cat icon near your clock.


IF IT WON'T OPEN AT ALL
-----------------------

Nothing happens when you double-click, or you get a message about only
installing apps from the Microsoft Store:

  Your laptop is in something called S mode. Lots of new laptops come
  this way. Turning it off is free and takes a minute:

     Settings  >  System  >  Activation  >  Switch to Windows 11 Home
     (look for "Switch out of S mode" and click Open, then Get)

  You only ever do this once. It doesn't cost anything and doesn't
  remove any of your programs. Note that it can't be undone afterwards,
  so if you'd rather not, that's completely fine -- just let me know.

If your antivirus quarantines the file, it's being over-cautious about the
way the app is packaged. You can tell it to allow the file, or again, just
let me know.
